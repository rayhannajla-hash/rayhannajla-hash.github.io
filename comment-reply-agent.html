How it works

Fetch, draft, hold, post

  1. It fetches only what's new. The agent checks your channel on a schedule and pulls top-level comments you haven't answered yet. It tracks what it has already seen, so nobody gets replied to twice and nothing is missed between runs.
  2. It drafts in your voice, not a brand voice. I feed it your actual past replies — the real ones you wrote — so it learns your length, your punctuation, whether you use emoji, how formal you are. It reads the video the comment is on, so answers are specific instead of generic praise.
  3. It stops and waits. Drafts go into a numbered queue delivered to your chat. Nothing has been posted. Hostile or sensitive comments are flagged and left undrafted by default — those are the ones an AI should never answer on its own.
  4. You approve by number. Reply "1 3 4" and those post. "skip 2" drops one. "all" clears the queue. Editing a draft before it posts takes one message.

Why approval-first

The version that posts on its own is cheaper. Don't buy it.

I can build fully autonomous replies, and I'll build it if you insist. But your comment section is the one place your audience can hear you directly, and an AI that answers a grieving viewer, a refund request, or a bad-faith troll without you seeing it first will eventually cost you more than the time it saved. The approval step takes about ninety seconds a day. That's the whole price of not finding out the hard way.
